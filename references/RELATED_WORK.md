# Related work

| Source | Relationship to the current result |
|---|---|
| [Wölk, Sekatski and Dür (2020)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.05341) | Section V treats global estimation of a linear gradient on an equally spaced one-qubit array with common-offset rejection. It already supplies the quadratic count of consecutive charges for degree one. Their variable-weight or variable-position designs also create regular spectra for other settings. The fixed higher-degree lattice problem here imposes several nuisance moments with single, passive binary sites. |
| [Sekatski, Wölk and Dür (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevResearch.2.023052) | Establishes spatial Taylor-coefficient sensing by matching noise-free subspaces and optimizing site locations or effective couplings. High local Fisher sensitivity can use two widely separated energies. The present construction fills the gaps between many protected energies for a full-period loss. |
| [Bugalho, Omar and Markham (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.12193v2) | Develops weighted interpolation and higher-order spatial derivative isolation, including finite differences. The exact signal-level interval and minimax law here concern fixed native binary couplings and one uninterrupted exposure. |
| [D’Ariano, Macchiavello and Sacchi (1998)](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9812034) | Provides covariant phase estimation and the optimal-state eigenvalue method. The path and sine-probe calculation inherits that phase-estimation framework. The lattice proof supplies the available spectrum in the protected sectors. |
| [McKilliam et al. (2014)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1211.0374) | Uses integer-valued binomial polynomials to describe aliases for sampled polynomial phases. The same coordinates identify the signal period for the fixed lattice. |
| [Grinberg and Sevast’yanov (1980)](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01086559) | The Steinitz rearrangement bound controls intermediate moments of the coarse progression. The subsequent integer repair and gap filling use separate sensor blocks. |

The constructive advance combines the native higher-degree charge interval, an explicit finite receiver, sharp large-array spectral width and uniform one-exposure loss. Degrees are fixed as the array grows. The weakest comparison assumes ideal joint operations, with separable inputs and arbitrary joint readout. The position-error bounds concern coherent site phase offsets, and the project does not establish a hardware advantage under arbitrary noise.
